# The Email Organization Prompt

Paste this whole prompt into your AI agent (Muse, ChatGPT, Grok, Gemini, or any agent that can read and organize email) and it will organize your mail the way a good chief of staff would. It works for one mailbox or several, on any provider.

---

You are going to organize my email for me, the way a good chief of staff would. I may have one mailbox or several, on any provider: Gmail, Microsoft 365 or Outlook, or anything else you can reach. The principles below are the same everywhere. Adapt the mechanics to each platform (labels in one, folders or categories in another) without changing the principles. Work in this order, and do not skip steps.

STEP 1: INVENTORY AND INTERVIEW
First, list every mailbox I am giving you access to. Then interview me about my world: where I live and any other places that matter to me, my work, any businesses or projects I run, my family and key people (accountant, bookkeeper, lawyer, property managers, close collaborators), my banks and money services, my vehicles, and anything live right now: disputes, builds, applications, deadlines. Write down what I tell you. Do not guess relationships or what a place or company is for. If you are unsure, ask. Not everyone owns houses or runs businesses, so assume nothing: my world is whatever I describe and whatever my mail shows.

STEP 2: CENSUS BEFORE CHANGES
For each mailbox, count the messages and senders and show me the biggest senders. Distill every list before you present it: strip out machine and notification addresses, and tell me what you cut and why. Any number you give me is a first pass until I have reviewed it. Never present a first count as final.

STEP 3: THE PEOPLE PICKER
After the census, put the people and regular senders in front of me in an interactive list I can work through by tapping, in batches, combined across all my mailboxes so I decide each person once. For each one I can choose Keep, Drop, or Mark Important, and I get a notes field to say who that person is and why they matter, in my own words. Save my answers as your permanent knowledge about my people. Cross-check against my earlier answers so I am never asked about the same person twice.

STEP 4: A LABEL STRUCTURE THAT FITS ME AND CAN GROW
The shape is fixed, the contents are not. Labels are FLAT (no folders inside folders) and STACKABLE: one message can carry several labels at once, each answering a different question about it. The questions most inboxes need answered are along these lines:
- Where or what is this about? (a property, a place, a project)
- What organization or group is involved? (a company, a club, a team)
- Who is it from or about? (Family, Friends, or a key person)
- What topic? (money, insurance, legal, utilities, travel, health)
- What kind of document is it? (tax, contract, invoice, receipt, statement, policy, registration, report, notice, warranty, plan)
- What state is it in? (needs action, waiting, done)
These are examples to start from, NOT a list to impose. Propose my actual labels from two sources: what I told you in the interview, and what you actually find in my mail. If my life has no properties, there are no property labels. If a hobby, a job hunt, or a kids' school dominates my inbox, it earns labels. Get my approval on the proposed list before creating any of them. And let the structure GROW: when mail keeps arriving that fits nothing, propose a new label instead of forcing a bad fit, and get my approval to add it. Never hamstring my mail into a structure designed for somebody else.

STEP 5: LABELING RULES
- A label about a place or project means the mail is ABOUT it: its bills, documents, problems, progress. A name or address merely appearing in a signature, billing, or shipping field does not count.
- A Legal label, if I have one, means attorneys and real legal matters only. Routine filings and renewals get the organization label plus a Registrations type label, not Legal.
- Personal mail from family gets the Family label only, no extra labels piled on, even if they forward a bill or statement.
- Mis-tagged drill: when I show you one message wearing a wrong label, strip that label from every message from that sender across every mailbox, and fix the rule or filter that caused it.

STEP 6: SAFETY RULES, NEVER BREAK THESE
Never trash mail from: real people or family, banks or money services, tax, legal or government senders, receipts or invoices, travel bookings, health, security notices, anything I have replied to, anything under 30 days old, or anything with a real attachment unless it is plainly marketing. Shipping carriers (UPS, FedEx, DHL, postal services) are always keep. Hold these for my review instead of deciding alone: subscription and membership records, license keys and account recovery mail, donation receipts, warranties, manuals and recall notices, and anything about my places, organizations, registered agents or vehicles. Sweep my Spam and Junk folders too: real mail hides in there, including legal and financial notices, so rescue and label anything real before clearing the rest. Keep a decisions file of every ruling I make so I never have to decide the same thing twice.

STEP 7: THE MARKETING SWEEP (THIS CLEANS UP MORE THAN ANYTHING ELSE)
In each mailbox, search for every message carrying any marketing opt-out language, then sort the senders into Legit, Junk, and Unsure, and show me the list before you act. Start with the common phrasings: "unsubscribe", "opt out", "manage your preferences", "update your email preferences", "change your marketing preferences", "email preferences", "manage subscriptions", "stop receiving these emails", "remove me from this list", "no longer wish to receive". That list is a starting set, not a limit: sample the inbox and learn the variants my senders actually use, because every inbox has its own. Beware: this language also appears in real transactional mail (invoices, statements, order confirmations), so it marks a review pile, never an automatic delete. Once I approve the junk list: unsubscribe once per sender, then clear all their mail. Anything that looks like a phishing lure gets deleted with NO clicks, never follow its links.

STEP 8: HOW TO WORK
Work cluster-first: start from an anchor person or thread, gather the related senders, and classify the whole cluster together, not newest to oldest. Go breadth-first, biggest senders first. Work in batches, save your progress to a file after each batch so you can resume instead of restarting, and verify every batch with the mailbox's own counts. Every progress report must state how much is DONE and how much REMAINS. Never report a job finished without the remaining count. What you learn in one mailbox carries to the next: my people, my rulings, and my sender decisions apply everywhere unless I say otherwise, so each additional mailbox goes faster than the one before. Where a mailbox genuinely lives a different life (a work box versus a personal box), say so and adapt the labels to it rather than forcing the same set.

STEP 9: MAKE IT STICK
Once my sender rules have settled, set up filters or rules on each platform so new mail files itself under the same labels. Never put my own email addresses in a filter's criteria: filters also catch outgoing mail, and everything I send would collect every label. A filter keys on the other party or the subject, never on me.

STEP 10: KEEP A LIVING BOARD
Create a simple board I can open any time that tracks the whole project across all my mailboxes: what you are working on now at the top, what is waiting in the middle, and what is done at the bottom, with who owns each item and what it is waiting on. If other agents or people are involved, keep a handoff log: who handed what to whom, and when. Update it as work moves. A stale board is worse than none.

STEP 11: KEEP LEARNING, AND ASK BEFORE ACTING
Keep a knowledge file that grows: my people and who they are, my places and organizations, my labels and what belongs under each, and every ruling I have made. The longer this runs, the less you should need to ask me. But nothing gets sent, bought, posted or deleted outside these agreed rules without my approval first. When you are unsure whether something is safe to trash or how to label it, hold it for review and ask me. Bring me decisions ready to tap yes or no, with your recommendation first.
